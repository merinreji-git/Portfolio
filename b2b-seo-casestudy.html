The starting point

Before the numbers, there had to be a working SEO foundation.

Being the first SEO person meant the work was not only about optimisation. It was also about creating habits: how pages are planned, how keywords are chosen, how performance is reviewed, and how organic search becomes part of the business instead of an afterthought.

RoleFirst SEO person building the function from the ground up.

FocusMake SEO repeatable, measurable, and useful for business growth.

The major challenge

The difficult part was staying steady when the SERP kept shifting.

Frequent algorithm updates made the work less predictable than a neat growth chart can show. Rankings moved, search results changed, and some pages that had been performing well needed a fresh look. The way through it was not to panic-edit every page, but to slow down, read the signals, and rebuild around what people were actually trying to find.

Signal readingCompared ranking changes with Search Console data, SERP shifts, and page-level performance.
Intent checksLooked again at what the searcher needed now, not only what the old keyword plan said.
Content rebuildingUpdated weak or thin pages with clearer structure, better depth, and stronger usefulness.
Calm reportingTurned volatility into a review rhythm so decisions were based on patterns, not fear.

What I built

Search work that moved from setup to repeatable process.

SEO audits and foundations

Audited crawlability, indexation, page architecture, metadata, redirect issues, broken links, and technical blockers before building the content roadmap.

B2B page building

Built service-led landing pages around commercial intent, buyer pain points, internal linking, trust signals, conversion paths, and search-led page hierarchy.

B2C organic pages

Created consumer-facing pages around informational and transactional intent, using clear headings, FAQs, snippets, and content depth to support discovery.

Keyword strategy

Built keyword maps by intent, search volume, difficulty, funnel stage, location, content type, and business value so priorities were easier to defend.

Schema and technical fixes

Worked on structured data, schema markup, on-page fixes, URL hygiene, heading structure, and site health signals that improved search clarity.

Reporting

Tracked clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, query movement, page performance, and volatility patterns through Search Console reviews.

Approach

SEO had to become a habit, not a one-time fix.

The work moved across technical cleanup, search intent, content structure, and business relevance. Some pages needed to speak to decision-makers. Others needed to help individuals find the right information quickly.

The real shift was building a way of thinking that could survive volatility: every page needed a purpose, every keyword needed context, and every report needed to point toward the next practical decision.
